**ITERATION 1 - (v1 2july; 2026)** 

A:   
The Stafford Beer Archive at Liverpool John Moores University has digitized most of its collections, offering insight into the development of cybernetic and management science thought — particularly the documents related to the Cybersyn Project in Chile (my case study). These records capture not only the project's development, but the intellectual process through which concepts like the Viable System Model (VSM) took shape. Digitization has included scanning handwritten notes, diagrams, and correspondence.  
B:  
While text search interfaces facilitate quicker access to the digitized Stafford Beer Archive, they only provide a record-by-record view. Currently no visualization exists to support relational exploration of these documents, making it difficult to trace how ideas, correspondence, and diagrams connect across the collection or gain an overview of the archive. By contrast, this thesis argues for representing archival collections like the Beer Archive through visualization which allows multi-perspective views on records.   
C:  
The potential of AI tools and LLMs to process archival documents and data, and to generate visualizations that support their exploration, is a rich research field for DH and VIS. How such tools could enable intuitive exploration of complex archives, helping to reveal how records relate to the whole, and how people or concepts are connected,  are still open questions for the field. D:   
Where such exploration has been attempted in other digital humanities projects, it typically faces several challenges: (a) using knowledge graph databases to represent heterogeneous archival materials, (b) developing effective chat-to-visualization strategies that allow scholars to query these materials in natural language, and (c) achieving automated visualization creation that can be applied at different scales across unstructured collections without extensive manual curation and data processing. These challenges apply to the Stafford Beer Archive, which currently lacks relational or exploratory visualization.   
E:   
This thesis addresses each of these challenges directly: adapting knowledge graph structures to represent the heterogeneous materials of the SBA, developing chat-to-visualization strategies for querying these documents in natural language, and designing an automated visualization approach that scales across the collection without extensive manual curation. In doing so, it advances VIS, HCI, and DH research by demonstrating how visualization, as a core HCI practice, is itself a means for knowledge discovery.
